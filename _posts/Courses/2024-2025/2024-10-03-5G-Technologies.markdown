<h2>PART C - Aspects Techniques Detailles</h2>

<h3 class="section-title">1. Evolution des reseaux cellulaires : de la 3G a la 5G</h3>

<p><strong>Chronologie des generations</strong> :</p>

<table>
<tr><th>Generation</th><th>Periode</th><th>Debit max</th><th>Technologie cle</th><th>Usage principal</th></tr>
<tr><td>3G (UMTS)</td><td>2001+</td><td>2 Mbps</td><td>WCDMA, HSPA</td><td>Internet mobile, video</td></tr>
<tr><td>4G (LTE)</td><td>2010+</td><td>100 Mbps - 1 Gbps</td><td>OFDMA, MIMO</td><td>Streaming HD, apps</td></tr>
<tr><td>5G (NR)</td><td>2020+</td><td>10-20 Gbps</td><td>mmWave, massive MIMO</td><td>IoT massif, URLLC</td></tr>
<tr><td>6G (vision)</td><td>2030+</td><td>1 Tbps</td><td>THz, IA, quantique</td><td>Holographie, jumeaux numeriques</td></tr>
</table>

<p><strong>De la 3G a la 4G</strong> :<br/>
La transition de la 3G (UMTS/WCDMA) vers la 4G (LTE) a marque un tournant majeur avec l'adoption de l'OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiple Access) comme technique d'acces, remplacant le CDMA. Cette evolution a permis une augmentation significative des debits et une meilleure efficacite spectrale, ouvrant la voie au streaming video et aux applications gourmandes en bande passante.</p>

<p><strong>De la 4G a la 5G</strong> :<br/>
La 5G (New Radio - NR) represente une rupture technologique avec trois piliers fondamentaux :</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>eMBB</strong> (enhanced Mobile Broadband) : debits tres eleves jusqu'a 20 Gbps</li>
<li><strong>mMTC</strong> (massive Machine-Type Communication) : connexion de millions d'objets IoT par km2</li>
<li><strong>URLLC</strong> (Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication) : latence inferieure a 1 ms pour applications critiques (vehicules autonomes, chirurgie a distance)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="section-title">2. Techniques de modulation : OFDM et au-dela</h3>

<p><strong>OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing)</strong> :</p>

<p>L'OFDM est la technique de modulation fondamentale de la 5G NR. Son principe repose sur la division de la bande passante en de nombreuses sous-porteuses orthogonales, chacune transportant une partie des donnees.</p>

<p><strong>Avantages de l'OFDM</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Haute efficacite spectrale grace a l'orthogonalite des sous-porteuses</li>
<li>Robustesse face au multi-trajet (fading selectif en frequence)</li>
<li>Egalisation simple dans le domaine frequentiel</li>
<li>Flexibilite dans l'allocation des ressources</li>
</ul>

<li><strong>256-QAM</strong> : 8 bits/symbole (conditions optimales)</li>

<p><strong>Limites de l'OFDMA pour la 6G</strong> :</p>

<p>Cependant, l'OFDMA presente des limitations pour supporter le nombre massif de dispositifs mobiles envisage pour la 6G. D'autres techniques de modulation sont envisagees pour les generations futures, telles que le multiplexage par moment angulaire orbital (OAM), qui exploite le domaine spatial pour transmettre simultanement plusieurs flux de donnees.</p>

<h3 class="section-title">3. MIMO et Massive MIMO</h3>

<p><strong>Principe du MIMO</strong> :</p>

<p>Le MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output) utilise plusieurs antennes en emission et en reception pour ameliorer les performances du lien radio :</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Multiplexage spatial</strong> : transmission de flux de donnees independants sur des chemins spatiaux differents, augmentant le debit</li>
<li><strong>Diversite spatiale</strong> : envoi du meme signal sur plusieurs chemins pour ameliorer la fiabilite</li>
<li><strong>Beamforming</strong> : focalisation de l'energie radio dans une direction specifique</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Massive MIMO en 5G</strong> :</p>

<p>La 5G deploie le massive MIMO avec des antennes comportant 64, 128, voire 256 elements :</p>
<ul>
<li>Gain de beamforming significatif (concentration de l'energie vers l'utilisateur)</li>
<li>Augmentation de la capacite du reseau par multiplexage spatial multi-utilisateur (MU-MIMO)</li>
<li>Reduction des interferences inter-cellulaires</li>
<li>Amelioration de l'efficacite energetique (energie dirigee au lieu d'etre rayonnee dans toutes les directions)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Beamforming</strong> :</p>

<p>Le beamforming est une technique essentielle en 5G, particulierement pour les bandes mmWave :</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Beamforming analogique</strong> : dephaseurs analogiques, un seul faisceau a la fois</li>
<li><strong>Beamforming numerique</strong> : traitement numerique complet, multiples faisceaux simultanement</li>
<li><strong>Beamforming hybride</strong> : combinaison analogique/numerique, compromis cout/performance</li>
<li><strong>Beam management</strong> : procedures de recherche, selection et suivi des faisceaux (beam sweeping, beam tracking)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="section-title">4. Bandes de frequences 5G</h3>

<p><strong>Spectre 5G</strong> :</p>

<p>La 5G utilise un spectre beaucoup plus large que les generations precedentes, divise en deux categories principales :</p>

<p><strong>FR1 (Frequency Range 1) - Sub-6 GHz</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Bandes basses (&lt; 1 GHz) : couverture etendue, penetration des batiments, IoT</li>
<li>Bandes moyennes (1-6 GHz) : bon compromis couverture/debit, bande phare 3.5 GHz (bande n78)</li>
<li>Largeur de bande : jusqu'a 100 MHz par porteuse</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>FR2 (Frequency Range 2) - mmWave</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Bandes 24.25-52.6 GHz (principalement 26 GHz et 28 GHz)</li>
<li>Tres haut debit (&gt; 1 Gbps)</li>
<li>Portee limitee (quelques centaines de metres)</li>
<li>Fortement attenue par les obstacles, la pluie, le feuillage</li>
<li>Necessite beamforming et deploiement dense de small cells</li>
<li>Largeur de bande : jusqu'a 400 MHz par porteuse</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Compromis fondamental</strong> :</p>

<table>
<tr><th>Critere</th><th>Sub-6 GHz</th><th>mmWave</th></tr>
<tr><td>Couverture</td><td>Large (km)</td><td>Limitee (100-300 m)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Debit</td><td>Moyen (1-2 Gbps)</td><td>Tres eleve (&gt; 5 Gbps)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Penetration</td><td>Bonne</td><td>Tres faible</td></tr>
<tr><td>Densite antennes</td><td>Moderee</td><td>Elevee (small cells)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Latence</td><td>Bonne</td><td>Tres faible</td></tr>
</table>

<h3 class="section-title">5. Architecture reseau 5G NR</h3>

<p><strong>Architecture 5G</strong> :</p>

<p>L'architecture 5G introduit des concepts fondamentaux de flexibilite et de virtualisation :</p>

<p><strong>5G Core (5GC)</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Architecture basee sur les services (SBA - Service-Based Architecture)</li>
<li>Fonctions reseau virtualisees (VNF) et conteneurisees</li>
<li>Separation du plan de controle et du plan utilisateur (CUPS - Control and User Plane Separation)</li>
<li>Support natif du network slicing</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>gNodeB (gNB)</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Station de base 5G NR</li>
<li>Decoupe en unites : CU (Centralized Unit), DU (Distributed Unit), RU (Radio Unit)</li>
<li>Interface fronthaul (eCPRI) entre DU et RU</li>
<li>Support du Dual Connectivity avec LTE (EN-DC)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Modes de deploiement</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>NSA (Non-Standalone)</strong> : 5G NR avec coeur 4G LTE (deploiement initial)</li>
<li><strong>SA (Standalone)</strong> : 5G NR avec coeur 5G (architecture complete)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="section-title">6. Network Slicing</h3>

<p>Le network slicing est une innovation majeure de la 5G qui permet de creer des reseaux virtuels dedies sur une meme infrastructure physique :</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Slice eMBB</strong> : optimise pour le haut debit (streaming 4K/8K, realite virtuelle)</li>
<li><strong>Slice URLLC</strong> : optimise pour la faible latence et la haute fiabilite (vehicules autonomes, industrie 4.0)</li>
<li><strong>Slice mMTC</strong> : optimise pour la connexion massive d'objets IoT (capteurs, compteurs intelligents)</li>
</ul>

<p>Chaque slice possede ses propres parametres de qualite de service (QoS), garantissant l'isolation et les performances requises par chaque type d'application.</p>

<h3 class="section-title">7. Technologies pour la 6G</h3>

<p>La presentation que j'ai realisee avec Samia Boukouiss portait sur les technologies cles de la 6G. Voici les principales technologies identifiees :</p>

<p><strong>Communication Terahertz (THz)</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Bande de frequence 0.1 - 10 THz</li>
<li>Debits ultra-eleves (potentiellement &gt; 1 Tbps)</li>
<li>Portee tres limitee (quelques metres)</li>
<li>Applications : realite virtuelle haute definition, holographie, communications intra-chip</li>
<li>Defis : attenuation atmospherique importante, composants encore immatures</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Intelligence Artificielle et Machine Learning pour la 6G</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Optimisation en temps reel des performances reseau</li>
<li>Gestion predictive de la congestion et des ressources</li>
<li>Routage intelligent et allocation dynamique du spectre</li>
<li>Auto-configuration et auto-reparation du reseau</li>
<li>L'IA comme composante native de l'architecture reseau (non plus un ajout)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Communication quantique et securite avancee</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Distribution de cles quantiques (QKD - Quantum Key Distribution)</li>
<li>Communications resistantes aux attaques par ordinateur quantique</li>
<li>Cryptographie post-quantique</li>
<li>Securite intrinseque du canal de communication</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Visible Light Communication (VLC)</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Utilisation du spectre de lumiere visible pour transmettre des donnees</li>
<li>Bande passante enorme (400-800 THz)</li>
<li>Applications : communication sous-marine, navigation interieure, LiFi</li>
<li>Pas d'interference avec les systemes RF existants</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Ultra-massive MIMO</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li>Tableaux d'antennes de plusieurs milliers d'elements</li>
<li>Surfaces intelligentes reconfigurables (RIS - Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces)</li>
<li>Holographic MIMO : controle continu du champ electromagnetique</li>
<li>Gain de capacite exponentiel dans les zones urbaines denses</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Autres technologies envisagees</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reseaux non-terrestres (NTN)</strong> : satellites LEO, drones, HAPS pour couverture globale</li>
<li><strong>Jumeaux numeriques reseau</strong> : replique virtuelle du reseau pour simulation et optimisation</li>
<li><strong>Sensing et communication integres (ISAC)</strong> : le reseau devient aussi un capteur (radar, localisation)</li>
<li><strong>Computing integre</strong> : convergence communication/calcul/stockage</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="section-title">9. Impact societal et environnemental</h3>

<p><strong>Applications societales de la 5G/6G</strong> :</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sante</strong> : chirurgie a distance, telemedicine haute definition, monitoring patient en temps reel</li>
<li><strong>Transport</strong> : vehicules autonomes (V2X), gestion intelligente du trafic</li>
<li><strong>Industrie</strong> : usines intelligentes (Industrie 4.0/5.0), robots collaboratifs</li>
<li><strong>Education</strong> : realite virtuelle immersive, formation a distance augmentee</li>
<li><strong>Agriculture</strong> : agriculture de precision, drones de surveillance</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Preoccupations environnementales</strong> :<br/>
Les presentations sur les impacts environnementaux ont mis en evidence :</p>
<ul>
<li>La consommation energetique croissante des reseaux mobiles</li>
<li>L'empreinte carbone de la fabrication et du deploiement des infrastructures</li>
<li>La necessite de concevoir des reseaux "green" et energetiquement efficaces</li>
<li>L'objectif de la 6G : reduire la consommation energetique par bit transmis d'un facteur 100</li>
</ul>

<h2>PART C - Detailed Technical Aspects</h2>

<h3 class="section-title">1. Evolution of Cellular Networks: From 3G to 5G</h3>

<p><strong>Generation timeline</strong>:</p>

<table>
<tr><th>Generation</th><th>Period</th><th>Max data rate</th><th>Key technology</th><th>Primary use</th></tr>
<tr><td>3G (UMTS)</td><td>2001+</td><td>2 Mbps</td><td>WCDMA, HSPA</td><td>Mobile internet, video</td></tr>
<tr><td>4G (LTE)</td><td>2010+</td><td>100 Mbps - 1 Gbps</td><td>OFDMA, MIMO</td><td>HD streaming, apps</td></tr>
<tr><td>5G (NR)</td><td>2020+</td><td>10-20 Gbps</td><td>mmWave, massive MIMO</td><td>Massive IoT, URLLC</td></tr>
<tr><td>6G (vision)</td><td>2030+</td><td>1 Tbps</td><td>THz, AI, quantum</td><td>Holography, digital twins</td></tr>
</table>

<p><strong>From 3G to 4G</strong>:<br/>
The transition from 3G (UMTS/WCDMA) to 4G (LTE) marked a major turning point with the adoption of OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiple Access) as the access technique, replacing CDMA. This evolution enabled a significant increase in data rates and better spectral efficiency, paving the way for video streaming and bandwidth-intensive applications.</p>

<p><strong>From 4G to 5G</strong>:<br/>
5G (New Radio - NR) represents a technological breakthrough with three fundamental pillars:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>eMBB</strong> (enhanced Mobile Broadband): very high data rates up to 20 Gbps</li>
<li><strong>mMTC</strong> (massive Machine-Type Communication): connecting millions of IoT devices per km2</li>
<li><strong>URLLC</strong> (Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication): latency below 1 ms for critical applications (autonomous vehicles, remote surgery)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="section-title">2. Modulation Techniques: OFDM and Beyond</h3>

<p><strong>OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing)</strong>:</p>

<p>OFDM is the fundamental modulation technique of 5G NR. Its principle is based on dividing the bandwidth into many orthogonal subcarriers, each carrying a portion of the data.</p>

<p><strong>Advantages of OFDM</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>High spectral efficiency due to subcarrier orthogonality</li>
<li>Robustness against multipath (frequency-selective fading)</li>
<li>Simple equalization in the frequency domain</li>
<li>Flexibility in resource allocation</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>5G NR Numerology</strong>:</p>

<p>5G NR introduces the concept of flexible numerology with different subcarrier spacings (SCS):</p>

<table>
<tr><th>Numerology (mu)</th><th>SCS (kHz)</th><th>Symbol duration</th><th>Typical bandwidth</th><th>Usage</th></tr>
<tr><td>0</td><td>15</td><td>66.7 us</td><td>Sub-6 GHz</td><td>Low-band eMBB</td></tr>
<tr><td>1</td><td>30</td><td>33.3 us</td><td>Sub-6 GHz</td><td>Mid-band eMBB</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td>60</td><td>16.7 us</td><td>Sub-6 GHz / mmWave</td><td>eMBB / URLLC</td></tr>
<tr><td>3</td><td>120</td><td>8.33 us</td><td>mmWave</td><td>mmWave eMBB</td></tr>
<tr><td>4</td><td>240</td><td>4.17 us</td><td>mmWave</td><td>Synchronization</td></tr>
</table>

<p><strong>QAM Modulations</strong>:</p>

<p>5G uses high-order QAM modulations to maximize throughput:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>QPSK</strong>: 2 bits/symbol, robust (used in difficult conditions)</li>
<li><strong>16-QAM</strong>: 4 bits/symbol</li>
<li><strong>64-QAM</strong>: 6 bits/symbol</li>
<li><strong>256-QAM</strong>: 8 bits/symbol (optimal conditions)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>OFDMA limitations for 6G</strong>:</p>

<p>However, OFDMA has limitations in supporting the massive number of mobile devices envisioned for 6G. Other modulation techniques are being considered for future generations, such as orbital angular momentum (OAM) multiplexing, which exploits the spatial domain to simultaneously transmit multiple data streams.</p>

<h3 class="section-title">3. MIMO and Massive MIMO</h3>

<p><strong>MIMO principle</strong>:</p>

<p>MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output) uses multiple antennas for transmission and reception to improve radio link performance:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Spatial multiplexing</strong>: transmission of independent data streams over different spatial paths, increasing throughput</li>
<li><strong>Spatial diversity</strong>: sending the same signal over multiple paths to improve reliability</li>
<li><strong>Beamforming</strong>: focusing radio energy in a specific direction</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Massive MIMO in 5G</strong>:</p>

<p>5G deploys massive MIMO with antenna arrays featuring 64, 128, or even 256 elements:</p>
<ul>
<li>Significant beamforming gain (concentrating energy towards the user)</li>
<li>Increased network capacity through multi-user spatial multiplexing (MU-MIMO)</li>
<li>Reduction of inter-cell interference</li>
<li>Improved energy efficiency (directed energy instead of omnidirectional radiation)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Beamforming</strong>:</p>

<p>Beamforming is an essential technique in 5G, particularly for mmWave bands:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Analog beamforming</strong>: analog phase shifters, one beam at a time</li>
<li><strong>Digital beamforming</strong>: full digital processing, multiple simultaneous beams</li>
<li><strong>Hybrid beamforming</strong>: analog/digital combination, cost/performance tradeoff</li>
<li><strong>Beam management</strong>: beam search, selection, and tracking procedures (beam sweeping, beam tracking)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="section-title">4. 5G Frequency Bands</h3>

<p><strong>5G Spectrum</strong>:</p>

<p>5G uses a much wider spectrum than previous generations, divided into two main categories:</p>

<p><strong>FR1 (Frequency Range 1) - Sub-6 GHz</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Low bands (&lt; 1 GHz): extended coverage, building penetration, IoT</li>
<li>Mid bands (1-6 GHz): good coverage/throughput tradeoff, flagship 3.5 GHz band (n78 band)</li>
<li>Bandwidth: up to 100 MHz per carrier</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>FR2 (Frequency Range 2) - mmWave</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>24.25-52.6 GHz bands (mainly 26 GHz and 28 GHz)</li>
<li>Very high throughput (&gt; 1 Gbps)</li>
<li>Limited range (a few hundred meters)</li>
<li>Strongly attenuated by obstacles, rain, foliage</li>
<li>Requires beamforming and dense small cell deployment</li>
<li>Bandwidth: up to 400 MHz per carrier</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Fundamental tradeoff</strong>:</p>

<table>
<tr><th>Criterion</th><th>Sub-6 GHz</th><th>mmWave</th></tr>
<tr><td>Coverage</td><td>Wide (km)</td><td>Limited (100-300 m)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Throughput</td><td>Medium (1-2 Gbps)</td><td>Very high (&gt; 5 Gbps)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Penetration</td><td>Good</td><td>Very low</td></tr>
<tr><td>Antenna density</td><td>Moderate</td><td>High (small cells)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Latency</td><td>Good</td><td>Very low</td></tr>
</table>

<h3 class="section-title">5. 5G NR Network Architecture</h3>

<p><strong>5G Architecture</strong>:</p>

<p>The 5G architecture introduces fundamental concepts of flexibility and virtualization:</p>

<p><strong>5G Core (5GC)</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Service-Based Architecture (SBA)</li>
<li>Virtualized (VNF) and containerized network functions</li>
<li>Control and User Plane Separation (CUPS)</li>
<li>Native network slicing support</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>gNodeB (gNB)</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>5G NR base station</li>
<li>Split into units: CU (Centralized Unit), DU (Distributed Unit), RU (Radio Unit)</li>
<li>Fronthaul interface (eCPRI) between DU and RU</li>
<li>Dual Connectivity support with LTE (EN-DC)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Deployment modes</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>NSA (Non-Standalone)</strong>: 5G NR with 4G LTE core (initial deployment)</li>
<li><strong>SA (Standalone)</strong>: 5G NR with 5G core (complete architecture)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="section-title">6. Network Slicing</h3>

<p>Network slicing is a major 5G innovation that enables the creation of dedicated virtual networks on the same physical infrastructure:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>eMBB slice</strong>: optimized for high throughput (4K/8K streaming, virtual reality)</li>
<li><strong>URLLC slice</strong>: optimized for low latency and high reliability (autonomous vehicles, Industry 4.0)</li>
<li><strong>mMTC slice</strong>: optimized for massive IoT device connectivity (sensors, smart meters)</li>
</ul>

<p>Each slice has its own Quality of Service (QoS) parameters, ensuring isolation and the performance required by each application type.</p>

<h3 class="section-title">7. Technologies for 6G</h3>

<p>The presentation I delivered with Samia Boukouiss focused on the key technologies of 6G. Here are the main technologies identified:</p>

<p><strong>Terahertz (THz) Communication</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Frequency band 0.1 - 10 THz</li>
<li>Ultra-high data rates (potentially &gt; 1 Tbps)</li>
<li>Very limited range (a few meters)</li>
<li>Applications: high-definition virtual reality, holography, intra-chip communications</li>
<li>Challenges: significant atmospheric attenuation, still immature components</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning for 6G</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Real-time optimization of network performance</li>
<li>Predictive management of congestion and resources</li>
<li>Intelligent routing and dynamic spectrum allocation</li>
<li>Network self-configuration and self-healing</li>
<li>AI as a native component of the network architecture (no longer an add-on)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Quantum communication and advanced security</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)</li>
<li>Communications resistant to quantum computer attacks</li>
<li>Post-quantum cryptography</li>
<li>Intrinsic security of the communication channel</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Visible Light Communication (VLC)</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Use of the visible light spectrum to transmit data</li>
<li>Enormous bandwidth (400-800 THz)</li>
<li>Applications: underwater communication, indoor navigation, LiFi</li>
<li>No interference with existing RF systems</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Ultra-massive MIMO</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Antenna arrays with several thousand elements</li>
<li>Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS)</li>
<li>Holographic MIMO: continuous control of the electromagnetic field</li>
<li>Exponential capacity gain in dense urban areas</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Other considered technologies</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN)</strong>: LEO satellites, drones, HAPS for global coverage</li>
<li><strong>Network digital twins</strong>: virtual replica of the network for simulation and optimization</li>
<li><strong>Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC)</strong>: the network also becomes a sensor (radar, localization)</li>
<li><strong>Integrated computing</strong>: convergence of communication/computing/storage</li>
</ul>
